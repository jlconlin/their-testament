Keeping them safe

Notes outlive us — but not forever.

A lifetime of marked scripture lives in a Church Account, and an account outlives its owner only for a while.

If nothing has been decided in advance, and no family member asks within two years of a death, that record can be deleted. There are two separate ways to keep it — one you can do today, and one that is still open to a family whose relative has already died.

While you can: say what should happen

At account.churchofjesuschrist.org/legacyContacts you can choose what becomes of your Gospel Library notes and highlights — including allowing your family and descendants to reach them after your death. It takes a minute. The same setting sits behind Settings on your notes page, if you happen to be there already.

It is worth asking your parents and grandparents to do the same, and offering to sit with them while they do. It is a small thing to arrange now, and impossible to arrange later.

If someone has already died

Their notes are not deleted the moment an account closes. A spouse, a parent, or the legal custodian of the estate can write to the Church's Data Privacy Office at DataPrivacyOfficer@ChurchofJesusChrist.org and ask for access.

Expect to give your own full name, date of birth and address; your relative's full name, date of birth and Church Account email address; and something showing your relationship to them. If the request is granted, the Church walks you through setting up an account holding a copy of your relative's notes and annotations.

That copy is what this site can then turn into a book.

All of the above is the Church's own process, and nothing to do with this site. We cannot retrieve anyone's notes, and we never see them. But a book can only be made from a record that still exists — which is why it comes first.
